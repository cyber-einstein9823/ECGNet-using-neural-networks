How the Five Heartbeat Types Differ in Waveform Morphology

Figure 1 (Generated via src/plot_beats.py) — First Training Example per Class:

Morphological Comparison (Task 1.5): Normal beats (N) show a sharp initial downward deflection followed by a low baseline, a small pre-peak hump, and a tall, narrow secondary R-peak around 650 ms before returning to the zero-padded baseline at ~800 ms. Supraventricular ectopic beats (S) exhibit a much broader, elevated mid-beat wave (~300 ms) prior to a sharp spike at ~700 ms, while Ventricular ectopic beats (V) lack the sharp initial drop and instead sustain a high, broad plateau between 150 ms and 700 ms capped by a late spike. Fusion beats (F) blend normal and ventricular characteristics with a lower twin-humped baseline and an earlier sharp peak at ~530 ms, whereas Unknown/paced beats (Q) dip sharply to zero at ~60 ms, rise into a broad rounded dome (~330 ms), and finish with a wide secondary complex extending to ~1,000 ms.

Task 3.2 Analytical Backpropagation Check (B = 64)

Hand-Derived Output Gradients vs. PyTorch Autograd

∂J / ∂W^[3] = (1 / B) · (Y_hat − Y)^T @ A^[2]   // shape (5, 64)
∂J / ∂b^[3] = (1 / B) · ∑_{i=1..B} (y_hat^(i) − y^(i))   // shape (5,)

Evaluated on the first 64 training rows before parameter updates. Comparing our manual matrix derivatives against model.W3.grad and model.b3.grad yields maximum absolute discrepancies of 2.98 × 10⁻⁸ and 5.96 × 10⁻⁸—more than 300× tighter than the 10⁻⁵ specification.

Task 6 Complete Analysis & Oral Viva Defense Reference

Six Core Engineering Questions (Click Any Question to Highlight or Expand All)

6.1 • ALWAYS-"N" BASELINE & WHY OVERALL ACCURACY MISLEADS

A trivial classifier that predicts "N" for every heartbeat achieves 18,118 / 21,892 = 82.76% test accuracy while missing 100% of life-threatening arrhythmias (balanced accuracy = 20.00%). Our neural network achieves 97.82% overall accuracy and 83.65% balanced accuracy—proving that on heavily skewed clinical data, per-class recall and balanced accuracy are mandatory.

6.2 • LOWEST PER-CLASS ACCURACY (CLASS F: 62.96%)

Class F (Fusion beat) has the lowest per-class accuracy at 62.96% (102 / 162). In the confusion matrix, it is most often mistaken for Class N (39 times) and Class V (21 times). Electrophysiologically, a fusion beat occurs when a normal sinus impulse and an ectopic ventricular impulse simultaneously activate the ventricles, creating an intermediate waveform between N and V, compounded by having only 641 training rows (0.73%).

6.3 • LOSS CURVES & GENERALIZATION GAP ANALYSIS

For α = 0.5, training loss drops smoothly from 0.177 to 0.050 across 30 epochs, while validation loss reaches its minimum of 0.0722 at Epoch 23 (training loss 0.0560, gap = 0.0162) before plateauing and fluctuating slightly between 0.072 and 0.080. The network is well-fit at Epoch 23 and shows only mild late-epoch overfitting, which checkpointing at Epoch 23 prevents.

6.4 • SYMMETRY BREAKING & HE INITIALIZATION √(2 / n^[l-1])

Initializing all weights to zero causes every neuron in a hidden layer to compute the exact same pre-activation and receive identical backpropagation gradients, preventing specialization. Because ReLU(z) = max(z, 0) zeros out negative inputs, it halves the variance of activations at each layer; sampling W^[l] ~ N(0, 2/n^[l-1]) compensates for that factor of 1/2 so forward activations and backward gradients neither vanish nor explode.

6.5 • MOMENTUM CANCELLATION & ZERO-GRADIENT WEIGHT DECAY

In v_P = β v_P + (1−β) g, opposite-sign gradients across consecutive mini-batches partially cancel each other out, damping oscillations across narrow loss ravines while reinforcing consistent descent directions. When a weight receives zero data gradient (∂J/∂W = 0), L2 weight decay still adds g = λW, shrinking the weight geometrically toward zero (W ← W(1 − α(1−β)λ) when starting from rest).

6.6 • REMEDY FOR RARE CLASS F (641 TRAINING ROWS)

Solution 1 (Loss): Replace unweighted cross-entropy with class-weighted cross-entropy L = -w_y log softmax(z)_y where w_k = N / (5 · N_k), giving Class F a weight of 27.32× vs. 0.24× for Class N so misclassifying a rare fusion beat carries 113× larger gradient penalty. Solution 2 (Data): Stratified mini-batch oversampling of minority classes S and F.
